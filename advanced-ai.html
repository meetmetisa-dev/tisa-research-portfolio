01

Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning

Cooperative Edge QoE Control

Can cooperative resource agents adapt joint service levels to time-varying demand while controlling congestion and retaining fair service?

0.886learned mean reward
+22.7%reward gain vs random
0.933mean simulated QoE
0.05%overload rate

Method

Centralized joint-action Q-learning over a transparent 27-state, 27-action synthetic environment, compared with random, demand-aware, and fixed-balanced policies using matched seeds.

Evidence boundary

Synthetic demand only; centralized joint-action learning is not scalable MARL. The strong fixed-balanced baseline remains marginally higher in reward and is explicitly disclosed.

02

Federated Learning

Non-IID Multisite FedAvg

How well does weighted federated averaging learn across heterogeneous sites when the least-served client is part of the evaluation contract?

76.2%mean client accuracy
73.2%worst-client accuracy
0.017cross-client std.
58.5 KiBsimulated communication

Method

Eight client-specific non-IID datasets, clipped local logistic-regression updates, weighted FedAvg, per-client holdouts, and communication accounting.

Evidence boundary

Local data retention is not a formal privacy guarantee; no differential privacy, secure aggregation, or poisoning defense is implemented.

03

Deep Reinforcement Learning

Double DQN for Drift-Aware QoE Recovery

Can a learned controller reduce outages and residual error while managing the cost and fatigue of recovery actions under changing conditions?

0.800mean simulated QoE
0.05%outage rate
0.112mean residual
+648.7%reward gain vs monitor-only

Method

A five-input ReLU Q-network with replay, online and target networks, Double-DQN targets, and comparisons to threshold, random, and monitor-only policies.

Evidence boundary

Synthetic environment only. The controller is educational, not production performance or safety certification.
